1 · What LOOM is

LOOM is a music studio that runs in a web browser. You write the notes, choose the sounds, shape the mix, record your voice, and share the result — but you can also start from a complete, editable song in a chosen style and change it until it is yours. Nothing is generated by a hosted AI service: the styles are written down as plain data and a small, deterministic generator, so a template does exactly what its description says and the same variation number gives the same song.

What it is good for

  • Songs of almost any style — 64 ready-made styles (country, soothing acoustic, retro, jazz, hip hop, house, rock, world and more) and 44 drum beats to start from or ignore.
  • Poems and spoken word — paste the words, choose a mood, and get a fitted music bed with no vocal part; read over it, let LOOM lower the music under your voice, export timed lyrics.
  • Sketching and arranging — a piano roll, a drum step grid, a song timeline with named sections, and a mixer.
  • Showing your work — a share link anyone can open without installing anything, with comments pinned to the exact second, a screen-and-voice walkthrough recorder and a searchable transcript.
  • Making it together — invite people to edit a song with you; saves sync, and if two people save at once nothing is overwritten.

What it is not

  • It is not a replacement for a full professional DAW: there are no third-party plug-ins, no multi-microphone audio editing, and the time signature is always 4/4.
  • Sounds are a mix of recorded samples (piano, guitars, strings, real drum kits and more) and synthesised instruments. Recordings are a sparse selection that LOOM pitch-shifts, so they are good, not a full multi-sampled library.
  • It cannot sing for you or read your poem aloud into the mix. Vocals are yours: record them or import a file.
Honesty noteThe automated tests in this project render every instrument and every style through the real audio engine and check the numbers (loudness, silence, clipping, pitch, timing). A computer cannot judge taste: please listen, and tell the software what you would change.

2 · Quick start — a song in five minutes

Open LOOM and create an account. Go to the address where LOOM is running (locally: http://127.0.0.1:8000), choose sign up, and use a password of at least ten characters that is not based on your email. Your first project is created for you.
Press play. Your first project is a complete lo-fi song. Press Space or the ▶ button. The red playhead moves; the meters on the track cards dance.
Pick a style. Click ✦ Genre templates, filter by family (try Country), choose a card, and choose whether to create a new project or replace the tracks of this one. LOOM writes a 16-bar song — intro, verse, chorus, outro — across drums, bass, mids, vocals and effects.
Change something. Each track card has a sound dropdown. Switch the guitar to Banjo; click the ♪ button to browse and hear every sound before you choose. Press ⟳ Vary for a new take on the same style.
Write a few notes. In the Pattern view choose draw, click on the grid to add a note, drag its right edge to lengthen it. Click a chord such as Am in the chord palette to insert a whole chord at the dashed cursor.
Save. Press Ctrl+S (the dot on the save button means unsaved changes). Everything is stored in your project; history keeps the last 40 saves.
Share. Click ⤴ share then Publish link. Send the link: it opens, plays and takes comments with no account.
TipYou cannot break anything: every edit can be undone (Ctrl+Z, up to 100 steps), and deleting notes, tracks or clips shows an Undo button.

Tutorial · Step by step

Every step below shows the real studio with red numbered boxes; the numbers match the list under each picture. Do the parts in order the first time. Each step says what to do, what you will see, and gives one tip.

Part A · Your first song (about 5 minutes)

You will create an account, hear the starter song, build a country song from a template, and swap a sound.

Step 1 · Create your account

  • 1 — Choose the sign up tab.
  • 2 — Type a display name (at least 2 characters).
  • 3 — Password: at least 10 characters and not based on your email.
  • 4 — Press the submit button.

Do this: Open LOOM in your browser (locally http://127.0.0.1:8000) and click Start / Open the studio, then fill in the form.

You will see: You land in the studio with a ready-made project called First room.

Tip: If you see a red message, read it: it names the field that was rejected.

Step 2 · Meet the studio

  • 1 — Play button (also the Space key).
  • 2 — Track list: Drums, Bass, Mids, Vox — one card per sound.
  • 3 — View tabs: Pattern, Steps, Arrange, Mixer, Words.
  • 4 — Close the welcome hint with ✕.

Do this: Look around before touching anything. The left column lists the tracks, the centre is the editing area for the selected view, the right column is the inspector for the selected track.

You will see: Nothing changes yet — this is just orientation.

Tip: Click the ? button at any time for the shortcut sheet.

Step 3 · Press play

  • 1 — Play / pause.
  • 2 — Position read-out: bar : beat : sixteenth.
  • 3 — The red playhead moves across the piano roll.

Do this: Click the play button or press Space. Press it again to pause; Enter stops and rewinds.

You will see: You hear the starter lo-fi song. Browsers only start audio after a click or key press, so if it is silent, press play once more.

Tip: Click on the ruler first to start from the middle of the song.

Step 4 · Open the genre templates

  • 1 — Family filters (Country, Jazz, Retro …).
  • 2 — into: new project or replace this project's tracks.
  • 3 — variation number — the same number always gives the same song.

Do this: Click ✦ Genre templates above the editing area.

You will see: A dialog lists all 64 styles as cards with tempo, key, beat and sounds.

Tip: Leave into on “a new project” if you want to keep what you have.

Step 5 · Pick a country style

  • 1 — Click the Country filter.
  • 2 — Click the Country Ballad card.

Do this: Choose replace this project's tracks in into, then click the card.

You will see: LOOM writes a 16-bar song — intro, verse, chorus, outro — with drums, bass, acoustic guitar, pedal steel and vocals.

Tip: Not what you wanted? Press Ctrl+Z.

Step 6 · Listen and read what was built

  • 1 — New track cards: each part has its own sound.
  • 2 — Piano roll shows the selected track's notes.
  • 3 — The project is named after the style.

Do this: Press Space. Click each track card to see its notes in the roll and its settings in the inspector.

You will see: A complete song you can edit note by note.

Tip: ⟳ Vary rewrites the notes with a new variation but keeps your sounds and mix.

Step 7 · Change a track's sound

  • 1 — The selected track card.
  • 2 — Sound dropdown — instant switch.
  • 3 — ♪ opens the sound browser.
  • 4 — Inspector: attack, release, tone, layer, EQ, effects.

Do this: Click the guitar track, then either pick a new sound from the dropdown or press ♪.

You will see: The notes stay; only the voice changes.

Tip: Choosing a sound from another element moves the track to that element.

Step 8 · Browse and audition sounds

  • 1 — Search box (try “banjo”, “steel”, “rhodes”).
  • 2 — Category tabs.
  • 3 — Click a sound to hear it; double-click to use it.
  • 4 — Use this sound applies it to the track.

Do this: Single-click to audition — it plays a short phrase suited to that sound (a chord, a bass line or a drum pattern).

You will see: The track now plays the new instrument.

Tip: 77 sounds in all; 31 use real recordings when recorded samples is on.

Step 9 · Try another take

  • 1 — Vary — new variation of the template.
  • 2 — Undo — brings the previous version back.

Do this: Click ⟳ Vary a few times and listen to each result.

You will see: Fresh notes and rhythms in the same style.

Tip: Anything you drew by hand on non-template tracks is kept.

Part B · Writing and editing notes

Draw notes, drop in whole chords with strums, shape loudness and automate a filter.

Step 10 · The piano roll tools

  • 1 — Tools: draw, select, erase (keys 1 2 3).
  • 2 — snap: grid resolution.
  • 3 — quantize, humanize, fit to key, ×2, clear.
  • 4 — The grid: time runs left to right, pitch bottom to top.

Do this: Click the Pattern tab and select a Mids track. The shaded rows are notes that fit your key.

You will see: You can already click the piano keys on the left to hear pitches.

Tip: Ctrl+wheel zooms the grid.

Step 11 · Draw notes

  • 1 — Each green block is a note; its length is its width.
  • 2 — Tool bar: draw is active.

Do this: With draw selected click an empty cell to add a note; drag its right edge to lengthen it; drag the body to move it; right-click to delete it.

You will see: Notes appear immediately and play on the next pass.

Tip: Hold Shift and click to select several notes.

Step 12 · The chord palette

  • 1 — The seven chords of your key, with Roman numerals.
  • 2 — play as: block chord, folk strum, fingerpicking, power chords …
  • 3 — riff: writes a 2/4/8-bar guitar riff at the cursor.

Do this: Choose a play as style first (try “Boom-chick” or “Fingerpicking”) and a length.

You will see: Nothing is written until you click a chord.

Tip: quality, inversion and octave re-voice the chord.

Step 13 · Insert chords

  • 1 — The roll: chords appear at the dashed cursor, which then moves on; click another spot to move it.
  • 2 — The palette: click each chord to insert it.

Do this: Click G, then C, then D. They chain one after another.

You will see: Full chords with the chosen strum pattern.

Tip: On a Bass track only the root is written — a quick way to follow your chords.

Step 14 · Shape the loudness (velocity)

  • 1 — Velocity bars: one per note; drag up/down to change loudness.
  • 2 — The lane menu: velocity or automation.
  • 3 — humanize: adds small random timing and velocity changes.

Do this: Drag across the bars to paint a rhythm of loud and soft notes. Select notes first to change only those.

You will see: Accented notes pop, soft notes sit behind.

Tip: Use humanize on stiff, machine-like parts.

Step 15 · Automate a filter

  • 1 — Change the lane menu to Automation: Filter.
  • 2 — Click to add points; drag to move; double-click to delete.
  • 3 — clear lane removes it.

Do this: Draw a curve that starts low and rises. Play the song.

You will see: The sound opens up over time. The same works for volume, pan, reverb and delay.

Tip: The dashed line shows the knob's own value when a lane is empty.

Part C · Drums, arrangement and mixing

Program a beat on the step grid, structure the song, and balance the sounds.

Step 16 · Program drums on the step grid

  • 1 — Each square is a sixteenth note; click to place or remove a hit.
  • 2 — fill with a beat…: 44 ready-made beats.
  • 3 — Move through bars with the page arrows.

Do this: Select a Drums track, open Steps. Drag across squares to paint hits; Shift-click to cycle accent / normal / ghost.

You will see: Hits sound the moment you place them, and the loop plays them back.

Tip: Pick a beat from the menu, then edit it.

Step 17 · Structure the song in Arrange

  • 1 — All tracks on one timeline; sections in the purple bar.
  • 2 — + section adds a named section.
  • 3 — duplicate copies the selected range after itself.
  • 4 — import audio adds a clip.
  • 5 — record vocal.

Do this: Click a section to select it, then press duplicate to repeat a verse, or cut out to remove it. clear on track mutes a range on one track only (a breakdown).

You will see: The whole song is rearranged while the section markers stay in step.

Tip: Drop a WAV/MP3 straight onto a lane to import it.

Step 18 · Balance the mix

  • 1 — The selected strip: fader, pan, filter, EQ, delay and reverb sends.
  • 2 — Master gain.
  • 3 — recorded samples: switch between recordings and the original synth sound.

Do this: Open Mixer. Set drums and bass first, add chords, then vocals; use EQ to clear space; use reverb sparingly on parts that should sit up front.

You will see: Meters move live while you play.

Tip: M mutes and S solos a track.

Part D · Turning a poem into music

Paste a poem, build a music bed, read over it and let the music duck under your voice.

Step 19 · Paste the poem and choose a mood

  • 1 — Poem text — blank line between stanzas.
  • 2 — Mood: soft acoustic, country porch, retro tape, jazz …
  • 3 — Reading pace: slow, natural or brisk.
  • 4 — Build into a new project or replace.
  • 5 — Build music for this poem.

Do this: Open Words, paste your poem and choose the options.

You will see: The status line shows lines, stanzas and the estimated length.

Tip: Too fast? Rebuild with “slow & dramatic”.

Step 20 · Read what was built

  • 1 — Every line has a time; click a line to jump to it.
  • 2 — The current line in large text.
  • 3 — Record my reading (microphone).
  • 4 — Import my reading (existing recording).

Do this: Press play. The music bed has an intro, a section for each stanza and an outro, with no vocal part.

You will see: The lines highlight in time with the music.

Tip: Use headphones when recording.

Step 21 · Duck the music under your voice

  • 1 — Ducking depth: how far the music dips.
  • 2 — Duck music under my voice.
  • 3 — Remove ducking.
  • 4 — Export lyrics .lrc
  • 5 — Export subtitles .srt

Do this: After your voice clip is in place press the duck button.

You will see: Each music track gets a smooth dip just before you speak and a return afterwards (shown as volume automation).

Tip: Moved the clip? Press duck again.

Step 22 · Check the arrangement

  • 1 — Voice clip (waveform) sits at the first line; sections follow the stanzas.

Do this: Open Arrange. Drag the clip to line it up, add fade in/out in the inspector.

You will see: The recording and the music bed line up.

Tip: Overlap clips and fade them to crossfade takes.

Part E · Share, save and export

Send a link, save your work, and export audio.

Step 23 · Open the share dialog

  • 1 — Tabs: link, walkthrough, feedback, team, summary.
  • 2 — Publish link.

Do this: Click ⤴ share, then Publish link.

You will see: LOOM takes a snapshot of your song and creates a link.

Tip: Edits stay private until you update the link.

Step 24 · Copy the link

  • 1 — The public link — anyone can open it without an account.

Do this: Copy and send it. Viewers can play the song, read lyrics, comment on an exact second and react.

You will see: You see their comments in the Feedback tab.

Tip: Turn off link deletes the page and its comments.

Step 25 · Save

  • 1 — save (Ctrl+S).
  • 2 — The dot means unsaved changes.

Do this: Save regularly. Each save is a version you can restore from project ▾ › Version history.

You will see: A “Saved (revision N)” message confirms it.

Tip: Collaborators: if someone saved first you get a merge dialog.

Step 26 · Export

  • 1 — export menu.
  • 2 — WAV mix (16-bit, 44.1 kHz).
  • 3 — MP3 mix (192 kbps).
  • 4 — Stems: one WAV per track.

Do this: Pick a format. Turn on loop first to export only the loop region.

You will see: The file downloads when rendering finishes (tens of seconds for big songs).

Tip: MIDI export keeps notes and section names but not audio clips.

Step 27 · Keep the shortcut sheet handy

Do this: Press ? any time.

You will see: A sheet lists every shortcut for playing, writing and editing.

Tip: The full list is in chapter 17.

4 · The five elements

LOOM organises every song around five parts. A good track usually has at least one of each, and the colour chips on the left panel add them. This is the “5-element formula”.

1 Drums2 Bass3 Mids4 Vox5 Effects
ElementRoleSounds
1 · DrumsRhythm and energy. Edited per drum lane (kick, snare, hats…).10 kits: Acoustic (real recorded drums), Rock, Jazz Brush, 808, Trap, Lo-Fi, Electronic, House, Linn (LinnDrum samples), CR-78 (Roland samples). 16 lanes: kick, rim, snare, clap, low/high tom, closed/open hat, crash, ride, tambourine, cowbell, congas, shaker, clave.
2 · BassThe low foundation; locks to the kick and the chord roots.Sub, Synth, Electric (sampled), Pluck, Acid 303, Reese, FM, Wobble, Slap (sampled), Upright (sampled), Square Sub.
3 · MidsChords, guitars, keys, melodies and leads — the harmony and the tune.Keys & mallets (Soft Keys, Rhodes, Grand Piano, Organ, Clav, Vibraphone, Marimba, Kalimba, Music Box, Accordion), guitars (Acoustic, Electric Clean, Overdrive, Distortion, Muted, 12-String, Nylon, Ukulele, Banjo, Mandolin, Harp, Pedal Steel), strings & winds (Strings, Violin, Cello, Pizzicato, Mellotron, Trumpet, Sax, Flute, Brass Section), synths (Warm Pad, Analog Poly, Pluck, Saw/Square Lead, Super Saw).
4 · VoxVoices: sung-style synth choirs, or your own recorded/imported audio.Choir Ahh/Ooh/Eee, Vox Lead, Vox Chop, Whisper, Humming, and Audio Clips (a track that holds recordings and imported files).
5 · EffectsTransitions and atmosphere.Riser, Downlifter, Impact, Noise Sweep, Laser, Texture, Reverse Cymbal, Sub Drop, Vinyl Crackle, Wind, Whoosh, Glitch.

77 instruments in all; 31 of them (plus three drum kits) play real recordings when recorded samples is on — chapter 10.

Changing elementPicking a sound from another element (for example a guitar on a bass track) moves the track to that element automatically. The inspector's element dropdown does the same on purpose.

5 · Pattern view — the piano roll

Pattern is where notes are written. The grid runs left to right in time (a bar is four beats) and top to bottom in pitch; for drum tracks the rows are drum names instead of pitches. Faint shapes behind your notes are “ghost notes” from your other tracks, so you can see how parts overlap and line up.

The toolbar

The ruler, cursor and keys

  • Ruler — click to move the playhead; Shift-drag to set the loop region. Section names from Arrange appear here.
  • Dashed insert cursor — where the next chord or riff from the palette will be written. Click in the roll to move it; it advances after each insertion so chords chain.
  • Piano keys / drum names — click to hear that pitch or drum with the track's sound.
  • Velocity lane — below the roll: one bar per note; drag to set loudness (notes under the pointer, or the whole selection). Switch this lane to an automation lane — see chapter 13.

Selection actions (inspector)

With notes selected the inspector shows: a velocity slider; transpose buttons (−12, −1, +1, +12 semitones); duplicate; delete; and arpeggiate (turns selected chord notes into an up, down, up-down or random pattern at 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T or 1/16).

6 · Steps view — the drum machine

Select a Drums track, then open Steps. Each square is a sixteenth note.

  • Click an empty square to place a hit; click a filled square to remove it. Drag across squares to paint on or off.
  • Shift-click to place an accent, or to cycle a hit between accent → normal → ghost (quiet).
  • Right-click clears a square. Click a drum name on the left to hear it.
  • Page arrows and show 1/2/4 bars move through long songs.
  • The fill with a beat… menu writes a ready-made beat over the whole song.

Hits that are not on the sixteenth-note grid (for example recorded from a keyboard) do not appear here, but they still play and they show in Pattern.

7 · Arrange view — the song timeline

Arrange shows the whole song at once. Every track is a lane; note tracks show a small picture of their notes, audio tracks show clips with waveforms.

Sections

  • The purple bar under the ruler holds sections (Intro, Verse, Chorus…). Genre templates and poems create them for you; + section adds one at the playhead (it asks for a name), rename renames the nearest one, ✕ section deletes the selected one.
  • Click a section to select it as a range (and jump to it). Drag across the bar to choose your own range, snapped to bars (hold Alt for fine control).

Range tools

ButtonWhat it does (all tracks unless noted)
duplicateInserts a copy of the range right after it and pushes everything after it later. Section markers inside are copied too. This is how you turn a verse into two verses.
cut outRemoves the range and closes the gap.
clear on trackEmpties the range on the selected track only, leaving the others playing — handy for a breakdown.

Audio clips

  • Import audio or drop files onto a lane: WAV, MP3, OGG, FLAC, M4A or WebM up to 12 MB each. A new Audio Clips track is created if the selected track is not one.
  • Move a clip by dragging it (also to another track); trim by dragging either edge; split at the playhead (Ctrl+E); duplicate; delete.
  • Overlap — clips may overlap freely. Fade one out and the next in (inspector: fade in / fade out) to crossfade. Overlapping clips on different tracks simply layer.
  • Click M / S in the lane header to mute or solo; click the lane to select the track.

Mixing order that worksSet volumes first with the drums and bass; add the mids until the chords sit comfortably; bring in vocals; then use EQ to clear space (cut low end on non-bass parts, soften harshness in the high band) and use reverb/delay sparingly — more on sounds that should sit back, less on sounds that should be up front.

9 · Words view — poems and lyrics

Turn a poem into a song bed

Paste or write the poem. Leave a blank line between stanzas. Up to 400 lines. The status line shows lines, stanzas and the estimated length in bars and minutes.
Choose a mood. Soft Acoustic, Piano & Strings, Country Porch, Retro Tape, Late-Night Jazz, Ambient Air, Melancholy or Hopeful. Each sets tempo, key, sounds and chord pace.
Choose a reading pace (slow & dramatic, natural, brisk) and a variation number (🎲 picks another) and whether to build into a new project or replace this project's music (your recorded audio tracks are kept).
Press “Build music for this poem”. LOOM makes an Intro, one section per stanza and an Outro. Each line gets as many bars as a natural reading needs at that tempo (at least one). The bed has bass, guitar or piano, a gentle second instrument and — in some moods — a barely-there pulse. It has no vocal part: the voice is yours.

Read it over the music

  • ● Record my reading — creates a Voice track, places the playhead at your first line, starts the music and records your microphone. Press stop when you finish; the take lands on the Voice track where your first line begins. Use headphones so the music does not leak into the microphone.
  • ⤒ Import my reading… — use a recording you already have; it is placed at your first line.
  • Drag the clip in Arrange if your reading does not line up with the sections.

Duck the music under your voice

Duck music under my voice looks at every voice clip and writes a volume curve on every music track: a smooth 1-beat ramp down to 60%, 45% (default) or 30% just before you speak, held while you speak, then a smooth ramp back up. Because the curves are ordinary volume automation, you can refine them in Pattern (lane “Volume”). Remove ducking clears them. If you move the voice clip afterwards, press the button again.

Lyrics everywhere

  • Click any line to jump to it. In the studio and on the public share page the current line follows the playhead.
  • Export lyrics (.lrc) gives time-stamped lyrics for music players; Export subtitles (.srt) gives subtitle timings for video editors.
  • The poem text and mood are saved with the project, so you can come back, edit the words and build again.
How accurate is the timing?Line times come from a syllable count and the pace you chose — a reasonable reader's pace, not an analysis of your recording. Treat them as a good starting grid and move your clip (or the sections) if your performance differs.

Recording a vocal or instrument

13 · Automation

Automation makes a setting change over time: a filter that opens through a build-up, a pad that fades in, a sound that drifts across the stereo field, or music that dips under a voice.

  1. Select a track and open Pattern.
  2. In the strip under the roll, change the dropdown from Velocity (notes) to Automation: Volume / Filter / Pan / Reverb send / Delay send.
  3. Click to add a point; drag a point to move it (it snaps to the grid in time); right-click or double-click a point to delete it; clear lane removes the whole lane.

Between points the value ramps in a straight line; before the first point and after the last it holds the first/last value. While a lane exists, the lane owns that setting and the knob is ignored for it (the dashed line shows the knob's value when there is no lane). Playback that starts in the middle of the song, or a loop restarting, begins at the lane's value at that moment. Automation is saved with the project and included in WAV/MP3/stem exports and on the public share page. Mute and solo always win over automated volume.

14 · Transport, recording and MIDI

Playing and looping

  • Press play from anywhere: click the ruler first. Home returns to the start (or the loop start).
  • Loop: Shift-drag a ruler to define the region, press ⟲ loop. Playback wraps without a gap; long clips and automation continue correctly across the wrap.
  • Changing tempo while playing re-times the song smoothly.

Playing notes

  • Computer keyboard — letters A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P ; are piano keys; Z/X change octave. On a drum track the same keys play the drums (kick, rim, snare, clap, toms, hats…).
  • MIDI keyboard — press 🎹 MIDI in and allow the browser's MIDI permission (Chrome, Edge, Brave). Velocity from the keys is used.
  • Recording — arm ● rec, press play, and play. Each note starts on the nearest grid step and lasts as long as you hold it. Recording works on pitched and drum tracks (not on audio tracks).

Importing MIDI

project ▾ › Import MIDI file… reads a standard MIDI file: each channel becomes a track (channel 10 becomes drums; others become bass, mids or vox depending on the program and pitch range), tempo is taken from the file, and the song is lengthened if needed (up to 24 tracks and 20,000 notes).

18 · Recipes

A country song with a country feel

  1. ✦ Genre templates › Country › Country Ballad (slow, strummed) or Honky-Tonk (fast two-step); pick “a new project”.
  2. Keep recorded samples on: the acoustic guitar, upright bass, banjo and real brushed or acoustic drums carry the style.
  3. Swap the lead for a Pedal Steel (slides into each note) or a Violin fiddle from the sound dropdown.
  4. On the guitar track use the chord palette with play as: Boom-chick to write your own verse; the bass plays root and fifth on beats 1 and 3.
  5. Add a touch of reverb to the steel and delay (a dotted-eighth echo) for a rockabilly slapback on guitar.

Poetry set to soothing acoustic music

  1. Words › paste the poem › mood Soft Acoustic or Country Porch or Piano & Strings › Build music.
  2. Play it. If the pace feels rushed, rebuild with “slow & dramatic”.
  3. Record my reading with headphones. Do one take per stanza if you like — place them in Arrange.
  4. Duck music under my voice (45% is a good start), then check the dip in Pattern's Volume lane.
  5. Add a gentle fade in on your first clip and a fade out on the last. Raise the reverb send on the Voice track a little for warmth.
  6. Publish a link (lyrics follow along), or export MP3 plus an .srt for a video.

Retro and tape-warm

  • Templates: Tape Dreams (hazy), Retro 70s Soul (Rhodes, funk bass, tambourine), Retro 80s Pop (analog poly, gated feel), Doo-Wop, Rockabilly, Retro Lounge.
  • Select a keys or pad track › inspector › filter · EQ · drive · tape › raise tape wobble & warmth to 30–60%. Add the Vinyl Crackle effect track for dust.
  • Try the Mellotron (tape strings) layered under a Rhodes using the layer control.

Jazz

  • Start from Jazz Ballad, Cool Jazz, Smooth Jazz or Big-Band Swing. Swing is already set (30%+ for swing styles).
  • Use chord qualities in the palette: maj7, min7, 7, 9, min9, 6 — and try inversions for smoother movement between chords.
  • Bass: Upright Bass walks in quarter notes; for a ballad lower the tempo to 55–65 BPM and shorten drums to brushes (the “Jazz Brush” kit).

Beat-first, hip-hop or dance

  1. Add a drums track, open Steps, or use the beat… menu (Boom bap, Trap, Four on the floor…).
  2. Add a bass (808 style = Sub Bass), then chords in Pattern with the palette.
  3. Automate the filter on the chords over eight bars for a build-up; add a Riser in the effects element before the drop.

19 · How production works inside LOOM

This chapter explains what happens between “I clicked a note” and “I hear a song”, and how templates, poems and sharing are built. You do not need it to make music — it is here so the behaviour is predictable.

19.1 The big picture

Browserstudio, audio engine, generators, editors (plain JavaScript, no build step)
⇄
FastAPI serveraccounts, projects, history, audio, shares, comments, collaboration
⇄
DatabaseSQLite (local) or PostgreSQL
  • The sound is made in your browser with the Web Audio API. The server never plays or generates audio; it stores and serves data (project JSON, uploaded audio, share pages).
  • A project is one JSON document: tempo, swing, key, scale, bar count, sections, lyrics and poem text, master settings, and a list of tracks. Each track has its element, instrument, notes, audio clips, automation lanes, mix parameters, an optional layer, and mute/solo flags. Saving sends this document; version history stores up to 40 snapshots.
  • Audio files are stored separately in the database and referenced by id from clips.

19.2 Making sound: the scheduler

Browsers are poor at precise timing from ordinary code, so LOOM uses the standard “look-ahead” technique. A timer wakes every 25 ms and schedules everything that falls in the next ~180 ms onto the audio clock, which is sample-accurate. For each track it schedules: notes (through the sample player or a synthesiser), audio clips (with fades, speed and time-stretch), and automation (as value ramps). Swing is applied when a note's time is computed. When a loop wraps, a second time anchor is added so scheduling continues seamlessly. On stop or seek, every track's output is disconnected, silencing already-scheduled sounds instantly, and fresh signal chains are rebuilt on demand.

19.3 Voices: samples first, synth as fallback

Notepitch, start, length, velocity
›
Sample available and loaded?
›
Yesnearest recording, pitch-shifted by playback rate; held sounds loop on a matched zero-crossing
·
Nosynthesiser for that instrument

Recordings are kept every fourth semitone, so no note is shifted by more than two semitones. Each instrument's loudness is normalised when it loads so recordings sit at the same level as synthesised sounds. Drum kits map each drum lane to a one-shot; lanes a kit does not have use the synthesised drum. The synthesisers are written in code: oscillator banks with filters and envelopes, FM for electric piano and bass, plucked-string physical modelling (Karplus-Strong) for guitars, formant filters for “voices”, noise for percussion and effects.

19.4 Effects and the mix

Every track owns a small chain of audio nodes (see chapter 10's diagram). The shared reverb is an algorithmic design (parallel damped feedback combs into all-pass diffusers) chosen because it costs a fraction of a convolution reverb; its damping filters are deliberately set so the loop can never grow. The delay time follows the tempo. The master bus has a compressor/limiter, a gain, and an analyser that drives the meters and the scope.

19.5 Offline rendering (WAV, MP3, stems, tests)

An export builds the same graph in an offline audio context and schedules the whole song at once, so it uses exactly the same voices, samples, effects and automation as live playback, then converts the result to WAV (16-bit) or MP3. Because the same code is used everywhere, what you hear is what you export.

19.6 How a genre template becomes a song

Recipekey, tempo, beat, chords, sounds, styles
›
Sectionsintro, verse, chorus, outro
›
Chordsprogression fills each section
›
Partsdrums, bass, mids, extra, vox, fx
›
Projecttracks + markers
  1. Recipe (a plain data entry in data/genres.json): tempo, swing, root note, scale, a drum beat id, a drum kit, the chord progression (scale degree, optional quality, length in beats), and for each part an instrument and a style name.
  2. Structure: a list of sections (name, bars, and which parts play — each part can be off, on, or “lite”).
  3. Chords: the progression is repeated to fill each section. Degrees are turned into real chords by stacking thirds within the scale, unless the recipe names a quality (maj7, min9…). A voicing function places the notes close together near a comfortable register.
  4. Parts are written by style functions: bass styles (root, root-fifth, offbeat, walking, 808, syncopated, slap, country boom-chick, shuffle, cumbia, amapiano and more), keyboard/guitar styles (block, sparse, comping, stabs, pad, arpeggios, folk/pop/ballad strums, fingerpicking, palm-mute chugs, power chords), voices (pads, chops), melodies (a hook motif that repeats, with a fresh answer phrase every fourth bar) and guitar riffs (pentatonic). Drums come from the beat table with fills and crash lead-ins.
  5. Effects: an impact on each new section and a riser before a big one; optional texture (crackle, wind) across the song.
  6. All randomness comes from a seeded generator: same recipe + same variation number = identical notes.

19.7 How a poem becomes a timeline

  1. Parse: stanzas are separated by blank lines; each line is counted in words and syllables (a simple English syllable estimator).
  2. Pace: at the chosen words-per-minute a line lasts syllables ÷ syllables-per-second + a breath. The mood's tempo gives the bar length, so each line is given the whole number of bars (minimum one) that covers that time.
  3. Layout: an intro of two bars, then each stanza as a section whose length is the sum of its lines, then an outro; the total is rounded up to an even number of bars (and capped at 64; lines that do not fit are reported).
  4. Music bed: the mood's recipe is run through the same generator, but with these sections, and with no vocal part; the bed sits lower in the mix so a voice has room.
  5. Ducking reads the voice clips' start and end positions and writes volume curves that ramp down one beat before and back up one beat after, merging clips that are close together.

19.8 Time-stretch (keep pitch)

LOOM uses WSOLA (waveform-similarity overlap-add): the audio is cut into overlapping 46 ms windows, spaced further apart or closer together than the original to change the length, and each window is placed at the nearby position that best lines up with the previous one so the waveform stays in phase. Pitch is preserved because the windows themselves are not resampled. A search on a down-sampled copy keeps it fast.

19.9 Sharing, comments and collaboration under the hood

  • A share is a row holding a random 192-bit token, a frozen copy of the project, an optional walkthrough video and a transcript. The public page and its media endpoints only ever expose that copy and the audio clips it references — other files in your project return “not found”.
  • Comments and reactions need no account, so they are rate-limited per visitor and per page, length-limited, stripped of control characters and always displayed as plain text.
  • Every project save sends the revision it started from; the server rejects a mismatch with HTTP 409 and tells the browser who saved last, which produces the conflict dialog. A small status request every few seconds reports the latest revision and who is present.

21 · Troubleshooting

No sound.Browsers start audio only after you click or press a key — press play once. Check the master gain and that the track is not muted or another track is soloed (lit S).
Sounds seem thin at first.Recorded sounds load in the background; until a sound is ready the synth plays. The note beside “recorded samples” says how many are ready.
A track's note is invisible in the roll.The roll shows only the selected track's range; scroll up/down, or select the right track. Drum tracks show drum rows.
Recording is late or has echo.Use headphones; then drag the clip a little earlier in Arrange (or set its start precisely in the inspector).

“Someone saved first”.A collaborator saved after you opened the project. Choose “keep both” if unsure — it never loses anything.

“Save failed: Project state is larger than 1000 KB”.Very large projects (tens of thousands of notes) hit the 1 MB limit; split the song into two projects.
